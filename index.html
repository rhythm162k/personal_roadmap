What I'm building

One job platform, many service businesses

The idea

A customer asks for a service, the owner quotes and schedules it, a technician does the job from their phone, and the customer tracks progress and payment. One codebase; each business is a config: branding, service types, currency, time zone, language.

React + TypeScriptNode + ExpressPostgreSQL + RLSDrizzleRedis + BullMQDockerAWS

Why this one

It reuses Kin CRM's multi-tenancy and permissions, and adds what a CRUD app lacks: scheduling, workflow, file uploads, reminders and a customer-facing side. Repair and home-service businesses are easy to find and easy to explain the product to.

What changes per business type

Business Service fields Special flows
AC repair Unit type, brand, issue, address Site visit, parts quote, warranty
Appliance repair Appliance, model, fault Pick-up or on-site, parts ordering
Cleaning Property size, rooms, frequency Recurring jobs, flat-rate pricing
All of them Request, quote, schedule, technician updates, invoice, payment record, reminders

Sellable v1

  • Customer submits a request with photos, no account needed.
  • Owner reviews, quotes, schedules and assigns a technician.
  • No double-booking, enforced in the database.
  • Technician updates jobs from a mobile web page.
  • Customer tracks status, quote and payment.
  • Invoices and manual payment records (cash, bKash, bank).
  • Email reminders as background jobs.
  • English and Bangla, any currency and time zone.

Decisions to explain in interviews

  • Modular monolith, not microservices.
  • business_id on every tenant table, enforced by RLS.
  • Server computes totals; money stored as integers.
  • Exclusion constraint prevents overlapping jobs.
  • Request state machine with an event log.
  • Notifications and payments behind provider interfaces.
  • Write each choice down as a short ADR.
Check my assumptions in Week 1I'm assuming small repair and cleaning businesses take requests by phone and WhatsApp and track jobs in a notebook. Field-service tools like Jobber and Housecall Pro already exist abroad, so my edge is price, Bangla, local payments and setup done for the owner. Two real conversations beat my guess.
Not in v1Native mobile apps, AI, GPS tracking, live payment gateways, route optimisation, microservices. They cost weeks and aren't needed to prove the product. AI comes after launch.
Real client work

Find the first customer while I build

Go direct first

  • Start with AC repair, appliance repair, cleaning and maintenance businesses near me.
  • Show a working demo on my phone, not a slide deck.
  • Offer a cheap or free pilot in exchange for weekly feedback, a testimonial and permission to name them on my CV.
  • Get the scope in writing: what's included, for how long.

Marketplaces second

  • Search Upwork, Freelancer and PeoplePerHour for booking systems, customer portals and admin dashboards.
  • Read the listings to learn what clients ask for.
  • Expect heavy competition on plain booking sites; a live demo of a service-management portal stands out more.
  • LinkedIn is for reaching owners and founders directly.
Cut lineIf Week 4 ends and the technician view isn't done, shrink Week 5 (simple line-item quotes, no PDF invoices). Don't stretch the schedule.
Pilot dataUse demo data until hosting, backups and privacy are settled. Don't put a real business's customers on credits that expire.
Free tier, honestly

AWS & a SPOF-proof path

The free plan is shorter than most tutorials say.New accounts get up to $200 in credits, but the free plan ends after 6 months or when credits run out, whichever comes first, and the account then closes unless I upgrade. Guides promising "12 months free" describe the old system. Open my account in Week 4, not earlier, and read my own end date on the Credits page in Billing.

Cost rules

  • Budget alert and MFA before anything else; never use root day to day.
  • Load balancers and NAT gateways bill by the hour. Run labs for a day, then delete.
  • Some services are restricted on the free plan; check each one on the Free Tier page.
  • Run Redis in a container on my instance. Managed Redis costs money.
  • Don't host a paying client on credits that expire. Price hosting into the offer.

Custom domain, end to end

  • Buy or borrow a domain (the registration is the only unavoidable cost).
  • Point nameservers to Cloudflare (free DNS) or Route 53 (small monthly fee).
  • Request a free ACM certificate.
  • Add DNS records for the app and CloudFront.
  • Force HTTPS and test with curl and browser devtools.
Deploy ladder

Climb it as money allows

Level 0 · Week 4: one instance, Docker Compose

Cheapest, and everything is a single point of failure. Fine for learning and a pilot.

Level 1 · Week 7: managed pieces

RDS with backups and a tested restore, S3 + CloudFront, ACM HTTPS, CD with OIDC, alarms.

Level 2 · Later labs: no single AZ or instance

Load balancer, two instances in two AZs, RDS Multi-AZ, then ECS Fargate. Short, torn-down labs.

Level 3 · when a customer pays

Autoscaling, read replica, WAF, Terraform, blue/green deploys, a written disaster-recovery runbook with real RTO and RPO.

AI-era engineer

Build, don't vibe

Six rules

  • Design first, prompt second. Schema, API contracts and state machines are mine; AI drafts around them.
  • Explain-back before ticking. Answer each week's question out loud. If I stall, I don't own it yet.
  • Read every diff. Small PRs even solo; never merge what I can't explain.
  • No feature merges without its test. I write the cases; AI helps type them. Break the code on purpose to watch them fail.
  • Write ADRs. One paragraph per decision in docs/decisions. They become my interview stories.
  • Keep a confusion log. Each Friday, turn the top three into videos to watch.

Skill map

Already solid: TypeScript, React, Node/Express, PostgreSQL, Drizzle, RLS, JWT and OAuth, Zod, Vitest and Supertest, Docker for dev, multi-tenancy from Kin CRM.

New here: CI/CD, Redis, queues, scheduling and time zones, AWS core (IAM, EC2, S3, RDS, CloudFront, ACM), observability, security testing, k6, Playwright.

After launch: Terraform, multi-AZ design, Fargate, payment gateways, RAG and MCP.
